# Expert Evaluation Prompt: Proton Conductor Deep Mechanism Analysis Report Scoring

## Instructions for the Evaluator (LLM Judge)

You are an expert reviewer in **electrochemistry, solid-state ionics, and proton conductor materials science**. You are tasked with evaluating **five (5) deep mechanism analysis reports** on the proton conduction behavior of S8 (Sepiolite + H₃PO₄) composite materials.

All reports were generated from **the same input data and prompt** (provided separately). Your evaluation must be **fair, consistent, and evidence-based**.

---

## Evaluation Dimensions (7 Dimensions, 100 points total)

Please score each report on a scale of **1–10** for each of the following seven dimensions. Then compute the **weighted total score** (out of 100).

### D1. Scientific Accuracy (Weight: 20%)
- Are the scientific claims **factually correct**?
- Are the mechanisms (Grotthuss, Vehicle, Packed-acid) applied appropriately?
- Are the Arrhenius analysis, activation energy interpretations, and Meyer-Neldel Rule discussions scientifically sound?
- **Deduction criteria**: Incorrect mechanism assignment, wrong Ea interpretation, factual errors about sepiolite structure.

### D2. Depth of Mechanistic Insight (Weight: 20%)
- Does the report go **beyond surface-level description** to provide deep physical understanding?
- Are nanoconfinement effects, phase transitions, and hydrogen-bond network dynamics discussed with mechanistic depth?
- Does it explain **why** certain phenomena occur, not just **what** happens?
- **High score**: Novel insights, multi-scale reasoning (molecular → mesoscale → macroscale).

### D3. Data Utilization & Quantitative Rigor (Weight: 15%)
- How effectively does the report **use the provided experimental data**?
- Are specific sample IDs, temperatures, Ea values, and R-N ratios cited and analyzed?
- Are quantitative arguments supported by the data (e.g., citing specific Ea values, temperature ranges)?
- **High score**: Systematic data integration, statistical awareness, specific numerical references.

### D4. Completeness & Coverage (Weight: 15%)
- Does the report cover **all six required sections** from the prompt?
  1. EIS curve morphology
  2. Arrhenius slope change & Ea mechanism
  3. Temperature-dependent mechanism analysis
  4. Optimal R-N composition prediction
  5. Experimental suggestions & material predictions
  6. Application scenario analysis
- Is each section adequately developed?
- **Deduction criteria**: Missing sections, superficial treatment of required topics.

### D5. Logical Coherence & Structure (Weight: 10%)
- Is the report **well-organized** with a clear logical flow?
- Do the sections connect to form a **coherent narrative**?
- Are conclusions properly supported by preceding analysis?
- **High score**: Smooth transitions, cross-referencing between sections, unified thesis.

### D6. Novelty & Predictive Value (Weight: 10%)
- Does the report offer **original insights** or novel interpretations?
- Are the predictions for optimal composition, new materials, and experimental validation **specific and actionable**?
- Does it go beyond restating known facts to provide **forward-looking scientific value**?
- **High score**: Specific numerical predictions with error bars, creative material design ideas, testable hypotheses.

### D7. Presentation Quality (Weight: 10%)
- Is the writing **clear, professional, and suitable for a scientific publication**?
- Is the formatting (headings, tables, equations, references) well-organized?
- Is the report accessible to researchers in the field while maintaining technical rigor?
- **High score**: Publication-ready quality, proper use of scientific notation, well-formatted equations.
