1. EIS Morphology and Electrochemical Mechanism Analysis

The Evolution of Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) morphology in S8 materials (Sepiolite-H₃PO₄ system) provides a critical window into the microscopic proton transport dynamics within the 0.5 nm nanochannels.

1.1 High-Temperature/Room-Temperature Quasi-Linear Morphology

At room temperature and high-temperature regions (T > 250 K), the Nyquist plots for S8 materials typically exhibit a quasi-linear shape nearly perpendicular to the real axis. This morphology represents a typical blocking electrode response or restricted diffusion process. At these temperatures, the thermal energy is sufficient to overcome the energy barriers for hydrogen bond breaking, allowing protons to migrate rapidly via the Grotthuss mechanism (structural diffusion). The hydrogen bond network formed by and possesses high connectivity and dynamic reorganization capabilities. The absence of a high-frequency semicircle suggests that the charge transfer resistance ( ) is negligible due to efficient proton exchange, and the impedance is dominated by bulk resistance ( ) and the capacitive effect of proton accumulation at the electrode interface.

1.2 Low-Temperature Semicircle and Phase Transition

As the temperature drops below 240 K, the EIS morphology undergoes a fundamental change, characterized by the emergence of a high-frequency semicircle followed by a low-frequency tail. This transition signifies the appearance of distinct charge relaxation processes.

According to data from samples such as S8-2-1-2 and S8-3-2-3, significant jumps in activation energy ( ) (e.g., from 13.6 to 69.3 kJ/mol) coincide with the appearance of these semicircles. This is attributed to the "freezing" or glass transition of the confined system within the 0.5 nm pores.1 In such extreme confinement, molecular rotation and self-diffusion are heavily hindered by steric hindrance. When the continuous hydrogen bond network breaks, proton transport shifts from structural diffusion to hopping between discrete acidic sites, where the semicircle radius represents the resistance of this hopping process.

3. Temperature-Dependent Conduction Mechanisms

3.1 Room Temperature: Grotthuss Mechanism

In the ambient region, S8 acts as a concentrated electrolyte. Sepiolite channels facilitate the formation of 1D water/acid chains.4 Protons transport through the exchange of charge between protonated water/acid species: . This shuttling mechanism requires minimal (< 0.2 eV) and allows conductivities to reach 15 mS/cm.

3.2 Low Temperature: Packed-Acid Mechanism

Below the phase transition point, the continuous network is physically "frozen."

  • Vehicle Mechanism Restriction: In 0.5 nm pores, the translation of entire hydronium clusters ( ) is nearly impossible due to the proximity of the cluster size to the channel diameter.
  • Packed-Acid Mechanism: Protons hop between fixed phosphoric acid sites. This "Packed-acid" behavior is highly dependent on the packing density (the R-ratio). Sample S8-3-11-2 demonstrates that once free water is frozen at -65.9°C, protons rely solely on these high-barrier site-to-site jumps.

4. Optimal Composition Prediction and Synergistic Effects

Based on the statistical analysis of 39 samples, the following optimal ratios are defined:

4.1 High-Temperature Zone (T > 240 K)

  • Optimal Ratio Prediction: R = 0.31 ± 0.03, N = 1.6 ± 0.4.
  • Mechanism: R 0.3 provides the best balance between proton source concentration and carrier mobility. N 1.6 ensures that the nanochannels are fully occupied while maintaining a capillary liquid phase between fibers to ensure connectivity.5

4.2 Low-Temperature Zone (T < 240 K)

  • Optimal Ratio Prediction: R = 0.28 ± 0.03, N = 1.8 ± 0.3.
  • Mechanism: A slightly lower R-ratio (higher water content) helps suppress the glass transition temperature, preventing premature network rigidity. Increased N-values help maintain "unfrozen" interfacial layers at the channel walls, preserving a conductive path even at cryogenic temperatures.

4.3 Synergistic Influence of R and N

The R-ratio determines the density of proton donors, while N determines the connectivity of the transport medium. If N is too low (< 1.2), the system behaves as a dry powder with no continuous path; if N is too high (> 4.0), the material loses mechanical integrity and becomes fluid-like.

6. Application Scenario Analysis

6.1 Suitable Scenarios and Advantages

  • Cryogenic Power Sources: The ability to maintain activity at -82°C (0.023 mS/cm) makes S8 ideal for polar or aerospace solid-state proton batteries (SPB).3
  • HT-PEMFC: The H₃PO₄ system does not require external humidification, offering stability in the 100-200°C range.
  • Hydrogen Sensors: Utilizing fast proton response and high surface area for high-sensitivity gas monitoring.12

6.2 Challenges and Industrialization

  • Acid Leaching: Ensuring long-term retention of H₃PO₄ within the pores is critical to avoid corrosion and capacity decay.
  • Mechanical Strength: With a Young's modulus of ~3.6 MPa, structural reinforcement is needed for large-scale applications.3
  • Standardization: Natural clay purity variations require strict beneficiation protocols to ensure consistent electrochemical performance.13
