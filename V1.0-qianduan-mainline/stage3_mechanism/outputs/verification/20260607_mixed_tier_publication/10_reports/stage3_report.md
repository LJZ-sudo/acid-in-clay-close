## Selected Mechanism
Two-population bound-water network with sequential activation: H2 best reconciles the strongest evidence layers at once: near-universal VTF preference at the whole-sample level, a nearly universal detectable low-temperature breakpoint, and the large shift from lower apparent activation energies at high/mid temperature to much higher ones at low temperature. That combination is most naturally explained if the attapulgite-confined acid-water electrolyte contains at least two dynamically distinct proton-conducting populations or environments: a more weakly bound, cooperative acid-water network dominating at higher temperature, and a more strongly bound interfacial population that becomes comparatively important after cooling suppresses the mobility of the labile network. This also matches the chemistry of an acid-water system confined in a high-surface-area clay, where interfacial hydrogen-bonding heterogeneity and bound-water fractions are highly plausible. H2 preserves the observed cooperative transport character at high temperature while also explaining why a separate resistive process becomes resolvable on cooling without forcing the entire dataset into a single-transition or purely compensation-law picture.
